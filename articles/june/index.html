The Complete Guide to June's Obscure Holidays

June opens summer with a genuinely wide mix — Go Skateboarding Day and World Giraffe Day both land on the solstice, National Doughnut Day honors WWI's “Doughnut Lassies,” and there's an entire day dedicated to slowing down and sauntering instead of rushing.

This guide walks through all 62, day by day, with a bit of history or context for each one and a link to the full write-up — complete fun facts, ways to celebrate, and sourcing — on its own page.

Why does June have so many obscure holidays?

A meaningful cluster of June's holidays are tied to the summer solstice specifically — Go Skateboarding Day, National Daylight Appreciation Day, and World Giraffe Day (a nod to the giraffe's long neck) all deliberately land on June 21st. The month also runs food-heavy in a specific direction: ice cream, fudge, pudding, and other cold or creamy desserts dominate as temperatures rise.

How to use this guide

Holidays are grouped by date below. Each entry gets a short original write-up — not a repeat of the fact sheet — plus a link to the full holiday page if you want the deeper dive: complete fun facts, ways to celebrate, and sourcing.

June 1

🦖 Dinosaur Day

The word “dinosaur” — meaning “terrible lizard” — wasn't coined until 1842, by British paleontologist Richard Owen, decades after the last non-avian dinosaur went extinct. Birds are technically their direct descendants, meaning dinosaurs never fully disappeared. Full holiday page →

😊 National Say Something Nice Day

Founded by former Alabama Governor Bob Riley, built on research showing that receiving genuine praise activates the same brain reward circuitry as receiving actual money. Full holiday page →

June 2

🏃‍♂️ Leave The Office Early Day

Runs counter to hustle-culture instinct: research consistently shows that fewer, more focused hours often beat longer workdays on actual productivity. Full holiday page →

🍗 National Rotisserie Chicken Day

Costco alone sells more than 100 million rotisserie chickens a year, famously holding the price at $4.99 as a deliberate loss leader. Grocery store versions are often cooked in specialized convection ovens handling dozens of birds at once. Full holiday page →

June 3

🥚 National Egg Day

Eggs contain all nine essential amino acids, which technically qualifies them as a “complete protein” — a status most single foods don't earn. Shell color is purely a breed trait, not a nutrition signal. Full holiday page →

🔁 Repeat Day

A novelty holiday with no clear founder or origin, built entirely around the joke of doing or saying something twice. Full holiday page →

June 4

🐱 Hug Your Cat Day

Cat purrs sit in the 25 to 150 Hertz range, a frequency band that's been linked to promoting bone density and healing. A group of cats is technically called a “clowder,” and they're capable of over 100 distinct vocalizations, compared to roughly 10 for dogs. Full holiday page →

🧀 National Cheese Day

The earliest known archaeological evidence of cheesemaking dates back more than 7,500 years, found in Kujawy, Poland. The largest cheese ever made weighed over 57,000 pounds, produced in Ontario, Canada in 1995. Full holiday page →

June 5

🍩 National Doughnut Day

Started in 1938 in Chicago as a Salvation Army fundraiser, specifically honoring the WWI “Doughnut Lassies” who fried doughnuts for soldiers in the trenches of France. The doughnut hole itself is credited to 19th-century ship captain Hanson Gregory. Full holiday page →

🍪 National Gingerbread Day

Gingerbread reached Europe in 992 AD, brought by the Armenian monk Gregory of Nicopolis. The gingerbread man specifically is often credited to Queen Elizabeth I, who reportedly had them baked in the likeness of visiting dignitaries. Full holiday page →

🥃 National Moonshine Day

The name comes directly from the practice of distilling under cover of night to dodge tax collectors and law enforcement. What was once strictly illicit corn whiskey — nicknamed “white lightning” — is now legally produced by licensed distilleries. Full holiday page →

June 6

🎬 Drive-In Movie Day

Marks the exact 1933 opening of the first patented drive-in, Richard Hollingshead Jr.'s “Park-In Theaters” in Camden, New Jersey. At their peak in the late 1950s, more than 4,000 drive-ins operated across the US. Full holiday page →

🪀 National Yo-Yo Day

Marks Donald F. Duncan's birthday, who bought the yo-yo patent from Pedro Flores in 1929 and turned it into a household name. The word itself likely comes from the Philippines' Ilocano language, where ancient versions were reportedly used as hunting tools. Full holiday page →

June 7

🍫 National Chocolate Ice Cream Day

Chocolate ice cream is actually older than vanilla — one of the earliest known frozen chocolate recipes appears in a 1692 Italian collection by Antonio Latini. It still ranks as America's second most popular flavor, permanently trailing vanilla. Full holiday page →

📼 VCR Day

Sony's Betamax launched first, in 1975, but JVC's rival VHS format won the format war that followed a year later. The very last VCR manufacturer on Earth, Funai Electric of Japan, didn't stop production until July 2016. Full holiday page →

June 8

🤗 Best Friends Day

Backed by genuine research linking strong friendships to better mental well-being, lower stress, and even increased longevity. Full holiday page →

🍸 Name Your Poison Day

The phrase started as bar slang, a colloquial way of asking someone what they're drinking, though the holiday extends the idea to any personal preference or decision. Full holiday page →

June 9

🦆 Donald Duck Day

Marks Donald's actual debut in 1934's “The Wise Little Hen,” where he first appeared as a side character before becoming Disney's most prolific star by film count. His voice was performed by the same actor, Clarence Nash, for over 50 years. Full holiday page →

🥧 National Strawberry Rhubarb Pie Day

Rhubarb is botanically a vegetable, though it's used almost exclusively as a fruit in desserts. Only the stalks are edible; the leaves contain enough oxalic acid to be genuinely toxic. Full holiday page →

June 10

🖊️ Ballpoint Pen Day

Marks László Bíró's 1943 patent — he was a journalist who noticed newspaper ink dried fast without smudging, and adapted that same ink for a pen. Full holiday page →

🧊 National Iced Tea Day

Its widespread American popularity is usually traced to the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair. It's South Carolina's official state beverage, and roughly 85% of all tea consumed in the US is served iced rather than hot. Full holiday page →

June 11

✨ Make Life Beautiful Day

Entirely unofficial, with a deliberately broad premise — noticing existing beauty and actively creating more of it. Full holiday page →

🌽 National Corn on the Cob Day

Corn is botanically classified as both a grain and a fruit, since it develops from the plant's flower and contains seeds. Each strand of corn “silk” connects to exactly one kernel. Full holiday page →

June 12

🍪 National Peanut Butter Cookie Day

The signature crosshatch pattern isn't decorative — it flattens the dough enough for even baking, since peanut butter dough is too dense to spread on its own. Full holiday page →

🦸‍♂️ Superman Day

Marks the cover date of Action Comics #1 in 1938, Superman's very first appearance. Early Superman couldn't actually fly — he could only “leap an eighth of a mile” and was described as fast, not airborne. Full holiday page →

June 13

🌱 National Weed Your Garden Day

“Weed” is a genuinely subjective label — many plants people pull, like dandelions and purslane, are perfectly edible and nutritious. Full holiday page →

💡 Random Acts of Light Day

A conceptual, grassroots observance closely related to the broader Random Acts of Kindness movement, without any formal founder behind it. Full holiday page →

June 14

🥃 National Bourbon Day

Congress officially declared bourbon “America's Native Spirit” in 1964. To legally carry the name, it must be aged in new, charred oak barrels and made from a mash bill that's at least 51% corn. Full holiday page →

🍓 National Strawberry Shortcake Day

“Shortcake” refers to a high-fat, crumbly texture, not a literal size — the same “short” used in shortbread. Recipes started appearing in American cookbooks by the mid-19th century. Full holiday page →

June 15

📸 Nature Photography Day

Established by the North American Nature Photography Association specifically to tie the art form to conservation, not just aesthetics. Full holiday page →

😊 Smile Power Day

Only one of the roughly 19 identified types of smile, the “Duchenne smile,” involves both the mouth and the eyes — the marker researchers use to distinguish a genuine smile from a polite one. Full holiday page →

June 16

🥦 Fresh Veggies Day

Carrots weren't originally orange — early varieties were purple, yellow, or white, with the orange cultivar only gaining dominance in the Netherlands during the 16th century. Full holiday page →

🍫 National Fudge Day

The first documented fudge sale happened at Vassar College in 1886, reportedly for 40 cents a pound. Mackinac Island, Michigan has built an entire tourism identity around it. Full holiday page →

June 17

🥦 National Eat Your Vegetables Day

The recommended adult daily intake sits at 2.5 to 3 cups, a target a significant share of the population reportedly doesn't hit. Full holiday page →

🐊 World Crocodile Day

Crocodilians as a group have existed for over 200 million years — older than dinosaurs. They also hold the record for strongest bite force of any living animal. Full holiday page →

June 18

🎣 Go Fishing Day

Many U.S. states and Canadian provinces run “Free Fishing Days” timed around this date, waiving license requirements to lower the barrier to trying it. Full holiday page →

🍣 International Sushi Day

Sushi technically refers to the vinegared rice, not the raw fish — raw fish served without rice is properly called sashimi. Much of the “wasabi” served outside Japan isn't real wasabi at all. Full holiday page →

June 19

⌚ National Watch Day

The first known wristwatch was made by Patek Philippe in 1868, for a Hungarian countess — well before wristwatches became standard. Full holiday page →

🚶 World Sauntering Day

Founded in 1979 by W.T. Rabe on Mackinac Island, Michigan, deliberately conceived as a tongue-in-cheek counter to the then-popular National Running Day. Full holiday page →

June 20

🍨 National Ice Cream Soda Day

Credited to Robert M. Green in 1874 Philadelphia, invented essentially by accident when he ran out of ice for his soda fountain and substituted ice cream instead. Full holiday page →

🥤 National Vanilla Milkshake Day

The word “milkshake” first appeared in print in 1885 — describing an alcoholic whiskey-and-egg drink, nothing like the dessert it means today. Full holiday page →

June 21

🛹 Go Skateboarding Day

Established in 2004 by the International Association of Skateboard Companies, and formally recognized by the U.S. Congress in 2007 via House Resolution 1290. The date is deliberate, timed to the summer solstice. Full holiday page →

☀️ National Daylight Appreciation Day

Deliberately timed to the summer solstice, the longest stretch of daylight in the Northern Hemisphere all year. The word “solstice” comes from Latin for “sun” plus “to stand still.” Full holiday page →

🦒 World Giraffe Day

Also timed to the solstice, chosen as a symbolic nod to the giraffe's long neck by the Giraffe Conservation Foundation, which launched the day in 2014. No two giraffes share the same coat pattern. Full holiday page →

June 22

🧅 National Onion Rings Day

A recipe for fried onions with parmesan appears in a British cookbook as early as 1802, an early ancestor of the modern version. Full holiday page →

🌳 World Rainforest Day

Rainforests cover only about 6% of Earth's surface but hold more than half of all known plant and animal species. Deforestation reportedly claims an area equivalent to 48 football fields every minute. Full holiday page →

June 23

🕊️ Let It Go Day

An informal wellness-community observance, and the concept of letting go as an emotional practice predates Disney's “Frozen” by a long stretch. Full holiday page →

💧 National Hydration Day

The adult human body runs about 55–60% water, which is why even mild dehydration measurably impairs mood and concentration. Full holiday page →

June 24

🧚‍♀️ International Fairy Day

Fairy folklore varies enormously by culture — benevolent nature guardians in some traditions, mischievous tricksters in others. Full holiday page →

🏊 Swim a Lap Day

Swimming engages nearly every major muscle group at once, and water's buoyancy makes it one of the few high-benefit workouts that's genuinely gentle on joints. Full holiday page →

June 25

🐟 National Catfish Day

Established by an actual 1987 presidential proclamation from Ronald Reagan, specifically to recognize the growing U.S. farm-raised catfish industry. Full holiday page →

🍓 National Strawberry Parfait Day

The word “parfait” is French for “perfect,” though the American dessert and the traditional French version barely resemble each other beyond sharing a name. Full holiday page →

June 26

🍮 National Chocolate Pudding Day

“Pudding” originally described savory, often meat-based dishes — the sweet dessert meaning only took over in the 17th century. Instant chocolate pudding was introduced by Jell-O in 1947. Full holiday page →

🥥 National Coconut Day

Despite the name, a coconut is botanically a single-seeded drupe, not a true nut. The word itself traces to a 16th-century Portuguese term meaning “grinning face.” Full holiday page →

June 27

😎 National Sunglasses Day

The earliest known sunglasses were made by Inuit people from flattened walrus ivory, cut with thin slits to block glare off snow. The first mass-produced modern pair was sold on the Atlantic City boardwalk in 1929. Full holiday page →

🌸 Orange Blossom Day

The essential oil extracted from these blossoms is called neroli, a genuinely prized ingredient in perfumery. The flowers themselves have long symbolized purity, which is why they turn up so often in bridal bouquets. Full holiday page →

June 28

🧋 National Tapioca Day

Made from the cassava root, a South American tuber, and the word “tapioca” comes from the Tupi language of Brazil. It's the same starch behind bubble tea's chewy pearls as well as classic tapioca pudding. Full holiday page →

🪓 Paul Bunyan Day

Started as oral tradition among actual lumberjacks in the late 19th century, only reaching print decades later. Tall tales credit him and his blue ox, Babe, with carving out real geographical features like the Great Lakes. Full holiday page →

June 29

🤗 Hug Holiday Day

A separate holiday from National Hugging Day, which lands in January — this one has no connection beyond the shared subject matter. Full holiday page →

📸 National Camera Day

The first permanent photograph, Nicéphore Niépce's “View from the Window at Le Gras,” dates to 1826 or 1827. The first digital camera followed nearly 150 years later, built in 1975 by Kodak engineer Steven Sasson. Full holiday page →

June 30

🌠 Meteor Watch Day

Deliberately timed to the anniversary of the 1908 Tunguska event, which flattened over 2,000 square kilometers of Siberian forest — the largest impact event in recorded history. Full holiday page →

✨ National OOTD Day

The acronym stands for “Outfit of the Day” and exploded in popularity on Instagram and fashion blogs in the early 2010s, though documenting daily outfits predates social media by decades. Full holiday page →

Frequently asked questions

How many obscure holidays are there in June?

62, spread across all 30 days — including three that all land on the summer solstice (June 21).

What's notable about June 21st specifically?

It's the summer solstice, and three separate holidays deliberately land there: Go Skateboarding Day, National Daylight Appreciation Day, and World Giraffe Day.

Are there a lot of dessert holidays in June?

Yes — National Fudge Day, National Chocolate Pudding Day, National Chocolate Ice Cream Day, National Vanilla Milkshake Day, and several more make up a good chunk of the month.

Where can I find the full history and fun facts for each holiday?

Every holiday name in this guide links to its own dedicated page with a longer write-up, sourced fun facts, and ideas for how to celebrate.
